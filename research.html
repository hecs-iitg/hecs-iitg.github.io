Questions first. Tools second.

HECS studies transport, ignition, combustion and thermal-safety phenomena across fuels and energy systems, with hydrogen as the central research thread.

Research scope

One chain, from release to consequence

The six themes below are not six separate subjects. They are positions on a single physical sequence: fuel escapes, spreads, finds an ignition source, burns, and releases heat and pressure. Select a stage to jump to the theme that covers it.

Pillar 01

Hydrogen combustion & propulsion

Hydrogen's fast chemistry, broad flammability range and low ignition energy create both opportunities and control challenges. HECS will investigate flame development, lean combustion, abnormal combustion and hydrogen-fuelled power systems.

Flame propagationPre-ignition & knockLean burnHydrogen ICEIgnition systems
Key question

How can hydrogen's high reactivity be exploited for efficiency while controlling ignition, heat transfer and abnormal combustion?

Pillar 01B

Hydrogen-fuelled engines & direct injection

Hydrogen's wide flammability limits and small quenching distance make port injection prone to backfire and pre-ignition, and its low density costs volumetric efficiency. Direct injection removes both problems and hands the engine a harder one: mixture formation now has a few milliseconds to produce a charge that is burnable, stratified where it should be, and free of the hot spots that start combustion early. HECS will work on injection strategy, in-cylinder mixing and the abnormal-combustion limits that decide how much load a hydrogen engine can actually carry.

H₂ direct injectionMixture formationPre-ignition & backfireInjection timingVolumetric efficiencyNOₓ control
Key question

What combination of injection timing, pressure and spray targeting produces a charge that burns fast and lean, without pre-ignition, backfire or knock?

Pillar 01C

Hydrogen safety, leaks & dispersion

Accidental releases couple compressible jets, buoyancy, atmospheric flow, mixing and ignition risk. The lab will develop validated tools for release characterization, ventilation, detection and mitigation.

Leak jetsBuoyant plumesCrosswindFlammabilitySensor placement
Key question

When does a hydrogen release transition from jet-dominated to buoyancy- or wind-dominated behavior, and how should safety systems respond?

Pillar 02

Alternative fuels: methanol, ammonia & natural gas

Each of these fuels earns its place for a different reason, and each one fails differently. Methanol arrives as a liquid, so it brings spray break-up, evaporation and wall-wetting with it, while its high latent heat of vaporisation suits lean and boosted spark-ignition operation. Ammonia is carbon-free but burns roughly thirty times slower than hydrogen and carries an NOₓ and slip penalty, so it generally needs a promoter such as hydrogen to be usable. Natural gas is the bridging baseline with a mature supply chain, and it is where methane number, knock limits and methane slip get decided. HECS will run these as one comparative programme rather than three separate topics, because the honest comparison is what a fleet operator actually needs.

MethanolAmmoniaNatural gasH₂ blendingSpray & evaporationNOₓ & slip
Key question

For a given duty cycle, which fuel, blend and injection strategy delivers stable, efficient combustion without simply relocating the emission problem?

Pillar 02B

Sprays, ignition & optical diagnostics

Practical combustion begins with fuel preparation and ignition. The lab's first major build is a spray and combustion chamber with optical access, used to resolve spray penetration, evaporation, mixing, flame-kernel growth and ignition sensitivity for gaseous and liquid fuels alike.

Schlieren imagingLaser ignitionSpark ignitionSpray dynamicsHigh-speed imaging
Key question

How do injection, ambient state and ignition method control the earliest stages that determine the later combustion event?

Pillar 03

Computational reactive flows & transport

CFD is used as a physics tool rather than only a visualization tool. Research will include species transport, turbulent jets, combustion, multiphase interaction and conjugate heat transfer, with systematic verification and validation.

Growth area

Thermal safety & future energy systems

HECS will extend its transport and combustion expertise to thermal-management and failure phenomena in electrochemical and hybrid systems, including vent-gas ignition and battery thermal runaway.
