Build capability around the science.

HECS is a new laboratory. This page intentionally distinguishes immediate computational capability from planned experimental infrastructure.

Initial capability

Computational modelling

CFD, reactive/species transport, multiphase modelling, heat transfer and reduced-order analysis. Software access will depend on IITG licensing and sponsored projects.

Planned build

Hydrogen flow & safety bench

Controlled leak/jet experiments, mass-flow control, concentration sensing, ventilation studies and optical flow visualization.

Planned build

Spray & combustion chamber

A constant-volume chamber with optical access and a single-injector mount: spray penetration and evaporation, spark and pre-chamber ignition, flame imaging and pressure-trace analysis across hydrogen, natural gas, methanol and ammonia.

Growth phase

Hydrogen & alternative-fuel engine

An instrumented single-cylinder engine for hydrogen direct injection and alternative-fuel operation, with in-cylinder pressure measurement, injection control and fuel-system conversion, as sponsored project scope allows.

Growth phase

Advanced diagnostics

High-speed imaging, Schlieren and shadowgraph systems, and pressure-based diagnostics as the laboratory matures.

Growth phase

Thermal safety

Bench-scale thermal-management and ignition/safety experiments for batteries and hybrid energy systems.

Development roadmap

Three capability phases.

Phase 01

CFD + foundational bench experiments

Workstations and software, gas handling, flow control, sensors and safe small-scale hydrogen experiments, alongside the design of the spray and combustion chamber.

Phase 02

The spray and combustion chamber

The spray and combustion chamber in operation: injector characterisation, spray and flame diagnostics, controlled spark and pre-chamber ignition, and the first comparative runs across hydrogen, natural gas, methanol and ammonia.

Phase 03

Hydrogen direct-injection engine and thermal systems

A hydrogen direct-injection engine, plus application-scale safety and thermal systems, developed through sponsored research and collaboration.
