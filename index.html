Hydrogen, energy & combustion systems.

HECS IITG investigates how fuels ignite, burn, disperse and interact with thermal systems, from hydrogen direct injection in engines to methanol, ammonia and natural gas, combining experiments and computation to develop cleaner, safer energy technologies.

Launching November 2026HECS IITG is being established at IIT Guwahati. Formal laboratory activities will begin following the PI's joining.

Founding PI

Dr. Dhananjay Kumar

Incoming Assistant Professor · School of Energy Science and Engineering · Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati · Joining November 2026

Principal Investigator

Experiments, modelling and cleaner propulsion.

The research foundation behind HECS spans hydrogen-enriched combustion, hydrogen and natural-gas jets, alternative-fuel sprays, ignition systems, engine modelling, hydrogen leak dispersion and Li-ion battery thermal safety.

Before joining IIT Guwahati, Dr. Kumar completed his doctoral research at IIT Kanpur and subsequently worked at the Engine Research Center, University of Wisconsin–Madison.

Research philosophy

Understand the governing physics, validate it experimentally, then translate it into cleaner and safer systems.

The lab will be built around questions that require both fundamental transport science and engineering-scale interpretation.

Recruiting the first cohort.

HECS starts at IIT Guwahati in November 2026. These are the problems the first students will work on — described properly, not as a topic list.

Project 01 · PhD or MS(R)

When does a hydrogen leak stop behaving like a jet?

Where a release turns from momentum-driven to buoyancy-driven decides where the flammable envelope sits — and so where a sensor has to be.

CFDSafety
Project 02 · PhD

Making ammonia and hydrogen burn stably together

The blend that ignites reliably and the blend that keeps NOx down are not the same blend. Kinetics first, then chamber experiments.

CombustionExperiments
Project 03 · PhD or MS(R)

Ignition systems that can keep up with low-carbon fuels

Spark ignition was designed around gasoline. Hydrogen pre-ignites, ammonia barely lights. The first milliseconds set everything after.

DiagnosticsIgnition
